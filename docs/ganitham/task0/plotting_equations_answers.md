# Plotting Equations — Answers with Reasoning

Check the **reasoning**, not just the answer. If your point or table differs, redo that one arithmetic step and find where the walk went wrong.

# Part A — Multiple Choice

**A1. B** — the address (2, 5) is read x first: walk **2** across, then climb **5** up. Option A plots (5, 2), a different point.

**A2. C** — truth test: put the pair into y = x + 2. (1, 3): is 3 = 1 + 2? Yes. The others fail: (1, 2) needs 2 = 3, (2, 1) needs 1 = 4, (3, 1) needs 1 = 5.

**A3. B** — y = 7 never mentions x, so x is free and y is locked at 7: (−2, 7), (0, 7), (5, 7)… all at height 7 — a **horizontal** line.

**A4. C** — x = −3 locks x and frees y: (−3, −1), (−3, 0), (−3, 4)… all 3 steps **left** of the y-axis, stacked vertically — a **vertical** line.

**A5. C** — y = 5x + 1 uses the letters only with fixed numbers (times 5, plus 1) — a straight line. A, B and D all multiply letter by letter, so they bend.

**A6. C** — same spotting rule in reverse: y = x × x + 2 contains x × x, so it is a curve. The other three keep letters and fixed numbers apart — all straight lines (x + y = 12 is the downhill kind, x = 4y is fed from y).

**A7. C** — y = 2 × 4 + 3 = 8 + 3 = **11**.

**A8. A** — the rule wants the two numbers to add to 9. 4 + 5 = 9 ✓. The others: 5 + 5 = 10, 9 + 1 = 10, 3 + 7 = 10 — all miss.

**A9. B** — the crossing point must obey **both** rules: y must be 3 and x must be 5. Address, x first: **(5, 3)**. Option A is the trap — it puts the letters in the order they were mentioned, not in (x, y) order.

**A10. C** — the number multiplying x is the climb per step right. Check with the table: x = 1 gives y = 4, x = 2 gives y = 7 — one step right, three up.

**A11. B** — feed y = 3: x = 3 × 3 + 2 = 11. The pair is x = 11, y = 3, written **(11, 3)**. Option A forgets that x is always written first.

**A12. B** — y = (−4) × (−4) = **16**. A minus times a minus gives a plus — this curve never dips below the x-axis.

**A13. C** — test each pair against both rules. (1, 1): on y = x ✓, and 1 × 1 = 1 ✓ — on both. (2, 2) fails the square rule (needs 4); (3, 9) fails y = x; (4, 2) fails both.

**A14. A** — the origin is where both axes meet: no walk, no climb — **(0, 0)**.

# Part B — Fill in the Blanks

**B1. 8** — y = 3 + 5.

**B2. 7** — feeding x = 0 kills the 4x part: y = 4 × 0 + 7 = 7. (Feeding 0 is the fastest way to find where a line cuts the y-axis.)

**B3. vertical** — x is locked at 6, y is free: the points stack straight up and down.

**B4. 6** — the pair must add to 10, and 10 − 4 = 6.

**B5. 25** — 5 × 5.

**B6. parallel** — y = x + 3 floats exactly 3 above y = x at every x, so the gap never closes.

**B7. 11** — x = 2 × 4 + 3 = 11.

**B8. 0** — a point on the x-axis has no climb at all: its address is (something, 0).

**B9. 0, origin** — y = 6 × 0 = 0, so (0, 0) is a true pair: the line passes through the origin.

**B10. curve** — letter times letter bends the picture; the ruler test fails.

# Part C — Draw the Graph

Every sheet below runs from −10 to 10 on both axes, exactly as you drew yours.

**C1.** y = x + 3 — add 3 to each chosen x:

| x | −3 | −2 | −1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |

Points: (−3, 0), (−2, 1), (−1, 2), (0, 3), (1, 4), (2, 5), (3, 6). All on one ruler edge — a **straight line**, climbing 1 up per 1 right, cutting the y-axis at (0, 3) and the x-axis at (−3, 0).

```graph
line -10 -7 7 10 | y = x + 3
dots -3 0 -2 1 -1 2 0 3 1 4 2 5 3 6
```

**C2.** y = 2x − 1 — double, then subtract 1:

| x | −3 | −2 | −1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | −7 | −5 | −3 | −1 | 1 | 3 | 5 |

Points: (−3, −7), (−2, −5), (−1, −3), (0, −1), (1, 1), (2, 3), (3, 5). A **straight line**, steeper than y = x: 2 up per 1 right (the multiplier), cutting the y-axis at (0, −1).

```graph
line -4.5 -10 5.5 10 | y = 2x − 1
dots -3 -7 -2 -5 -1 -3 0 -1 1 1 2 3 3 5
```

**C3.** y = 5 is **horizontal** (x free, y locked at height 5). x = 2 is **vertical** (y free, x locked 2 steps right). The crossing point must obey both rules at once — y is 5 **and** x is 2 — so it is **(2, 5)**.

```graph
line -10 5 10 5 | y = 5
line 2 -10 2 10 | x = 2
dots 2 5 | (2, 5)
```

**C4.** x + y = 5 — whatever x takes, y keeps the rest of 5:

| x | −3 | −2 | −1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 8 | 7 | 6 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 |

Points: (−3, 8), (−2, 7), (−1, 6), (0, 5), (1, 4), (2, 3), (3, 2). A **straight downhill line** — 1 down per 1 right — cutting the y-axis at (0, 5), and heading for (5, 0) on the x-axis just past your last dot.

```graph
line -5 10 10 -5 | x + y = 5
dots -3 8 -2 7 -1 6 0 5 1 4 2 3 3 2
```

**C5.** x = 2y + 1 — feed **y** (it sits inside the arithmetic), collect x:

| y | −3 | −2 | −1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| x | −5 | −3 | −1 | 1 | 3 | 5 | 7 |

Points, x written first: (−5, −3), (−3, −2), (−1, −1), (1, 0), (3, 1), (5, 2), (7, 3). A **straight, gentle line**: 2 steps right per 1 step up (the 2 that multiplies y). The common mistake here is plotting the table columns as (y, x) — check your (1, 0): it must sit **on the x-axis**, one step right of the origin.

```graph
line -10 -5.5 10 4.5 | x = 2y + 1
dots -5 -3 -3 -2 -1 -1 1 0 3 1 5 2 7 3
```

**C6.** y = x × x − 4 — each x times itself, then subtract 4:

| x | −3 | −2 | −1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 5 | 0 | −3 | −4 | −3 | 0 | 5 |

Points: (−3, 5), (−2, 0), (−1, −3), (0, −4), (1, −3), (2, 0), (3, 5). The ruler test fails — (1, −3), (2, 0), (3, 5) refuse one straight edge — so join freehand: a **U-shaped curve** with its lowest point at (0, −4), symmetric left and right (minus times minus gives plus, so −3 and 3 land at the same height 5). This is the worksheet's U dragged **4 below** the x-axis — it now cuts the x-axis twice, at (−2, 0) and (2, 0).

```graph
curve -3 5 -2 0 -1 -3 0 -4 1 -3 2 0 3 5 | y = x × x − 4
dots -3 5 -2 0 -1 -3 0 -4 1 -3 2 0 3 5
```

**C7.** x = y × y + 1 — feed y, and expect a bend (letter times letter):

| y | −3 | −2 | −1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| x | 10 | 5 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 5 | 10 |

Points, x written first: (10, −3), (5, −2), (2, −1), (1, 0), (2, 1), (5, 2), (10, 3). A **U-shaped curve lying on its side**, opening to the right, with its tip at (1, 0). It never goes left of x = 1, because y × y is never negative — and x = 5 happens twice, at y = 2 and y = −2.

```graph
curve 10 -3 5 -2 2 -1 1 0 2 1 5 2 10 3 | x = y × y + 1
dots 10 -3 5 -2 2 -1 1 0 2 1 5 2 10 3
```

**C8.** Sort by one look: fixed numbers only → line; letter × letter → curve.

**Straight lines:**

- (a) y = 7 — no x at all: horizontal line at height 7.
- (b) x = y + 4 — y with fixed numbers only; fed from y, 1 right per 1 up.
- (d) x + y = 10 — letters added, no letter × letter: the downhill sum-line from (0, 10) to (10, 0).
- (e) y = 3x — x times a fixed 3: a steep line through the origin (x = 0 gives y = 0).

**Curves:**

- (c) y = x × x + 2 — contains x × x: the U-curve, lifted 2, lowest point (0, 2).
- (f) x = y × y − 1 — contains y × y: the sideways U, tip at (−1, 0), opening right.
