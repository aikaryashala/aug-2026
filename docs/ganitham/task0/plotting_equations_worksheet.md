# Plotting Equations — From Rule to Picture

**Goal.** An equation like `y = x + 2` looks like a piece of arithmetic. But it is also a **picture** waiting to be drawn. This sheet teaches you the table method: choose values, compute partners, plot points on graph paper — and watch a straight line (or a curve!) appear out of a rule.

**You need:** a notebook with graph (square-ruled) pages, a pencil, and a ruler (scale).

This sheet is written like a song: one **pallavi** (పల్లవి) — the rule we return to after every verse — and a series of **charanams** (చరణాలు) — verses that each add one new idea.

> **Pallavi — the golden rule of plotting**
> An equation is a **rule about x and y**.
> Every pair (x, y) that makes the rule **true** is one point of the picture.
> Plot enough true pairs, and the picture draws itself.

---

## Charanam 1 — The graph sheet and the address of a point

**a. What we set up**

On a graph page, draw two lines that cross:

- one going **across** — the **x-axis**;
- one going **up** — the **y-axis**.

The crossing point is the **origin**. Its address is (0, 0). Number the lines outward from it: to the right and upward the numbers grow 1, 2, 3, …; to the left and downward they go −1, −2, −3, …

In this task every graph sheet runs from **−10 to 10** on both axes — draw yours the same way, with the origin in the middle.

Every point on the page now has an **address** written as (x, y): from the origin, walk **x** steps across, then climb **y** steps up. The point (3, 2) looks like this:

```graph
dots 3 2 | (3, 2)
```

A minus in the address just reverses the walk: (−2, 1) means 2 steps **left**, then 1 up. (1, −2) means 1 step right, then 2 **down**.

**b. Task**

On one graph page, plot and label these points:

(3, 2), (2, 3), (0, 0), (−2, 1), (1, −2), (0, 3), (3, 0)

**c. Observation (what you should find)**

```graph
dots 3 2 | (3, 2)
dots 2 3 | (2, 3)
dots 0 0 | (0, 0)
dots -2 1 | (−2, 1)
dots 1 -2 | (1, −2)
dots 0 3 | (0, 3)
dots 3 0 | (3, 0)
```

- (3, 2) and (2, 3) land in **different places**. The order inside the brackets matters: first the across-walk, then the up-climb.
- (0, 3) sits **on the y-axis** (no across-walk at all), and (3, 0) sits **on the x-axis** (no climb).
- (0, 0) is the origin itself.

**Takeaway to say out loud:** "A point's address is (x, y) — walk x across, then climb y up."

## Charanam 2 — y = x: the rule becomes a line

**a. What we set up**

Take the simplest rule of all:

y = x — "y is the same number as x."

Which pairs make it true? (1, 1) does. (5, 5) does. (2, 7) does **not** — 7 is not the same as 2. To collect true pairs in an orderly way we build a **table**: choose easy values for x, and let the rule fill in y.

| x | −3 | −2 | −1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | −3 | −2 | −1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |

**b. Task**

Copy the table into your notebook. Plot all 7 points: (−3, −3), (−2, −2), … (3, 3). Now lay your ruler along the points.

**c. Observation (what you should find)**

```graph
line -10 -10 10 10 | y = x
dots -3 -3 -2 -2 -1 -1 0 0 1 1 2 2 3 3
```

All 7 points sit on **one straight edge of the ruler**. Join them: this is the **line** of y = x. It passes through the origin and climbs one step up for every step right. And it does not stop at your 7 points — the pair (10, 10) is also true, and so is every pair in between. A line is **all** the true pairs at once — that is why the drawn line runs right across the sheet, past your dots on both sides.

**Takeaway to say out loud:** "Choose x, compute y, plot the pair — the rule draws itself."

## Charanam 3 — y = x + 2: adding a number lifts the line

**a. What we set up**

Change the rule a little:

y = x + 2 — "y is 2 more than x."

| x | −3 | −2 | −1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | −1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |

**b. Task**

Plot these 7 points **on the same page** as Charanam 2's line. Join them with the ruler.

**c. Observation (what you should find)**

```graph
line -10 -10 10 10 | y = x
line -10 -8 8 10 | y = x + 2
dots -3 -1 -2 0 -1 1 0 2 1 3 2 4 3 5
```

Another straight line — and every one of its points floats **exactly 2 above** the y = x line. The two lines run side by side forever with the same gap: they are **parallel**. They never meet, because y can never be both "x" and "x + 2" at the same time.

**Takeaway to say out loud:** "Adding a fixed number lifts the whole line — same climb, new height."

---

## Charanam 4 — y = 4 and x = 4: when a letter is missing

**a. What we set up**

Two strange-looking rules:

y = 4 and x = 4

The rule y = 4 does not mention x at all. So x is **free** — take any x you like, the rule only insists that y is 4:

| x | −2 | 0 | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 |

Likewise x = 4 leaves y free: (4, −1), (4, 0), (4, 2), (4, 5) are all true.

**b. Task**

Plot both rules on one page. Then answer on paper: at which point do the two lines cross?

**c. Observation (what you should find)**

```graph
line -10 4 10 4 | y = 4
line 4 -10 4 10 | x = 4
dots 4 4 | (4, 4)
```

- y = 4 is a **flat, horizontal** line, 4 steps above the x-axis.
- x = 4 is a **standing, vertical** line, 4 steps to the right of the y-axis.
- They cross where **both** rules are true at once: at (4, 4).

**Takeaway to say out loud:** "The missing letter is free; the named letter is locked."

## Charanam 5 — x + y = 4: the rule need not start with "y ="

**a. What we set up**

x + y = 4 — "the two numbers add up to 4."

No side says "y =", and that is fine — the pallavi only asks which pairs make the rule **true**. Two numbers adding to 4? Easy to list:

| x | −1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 | −1 |

**b. Task**

Check three of the pairs by adding (does −1 + 5 make 4?). Then plot all 7 points and lay the ruler.

**c. Observation (what you should find)**

```graph
line -6 10 10 -6 | x + y = 4
dots -1 5 0 4 1 3 2 2 3 1 4 0 5 -1
```

A straight line again — but this one goes **downhill**: each step right costs one step down, because whatever x gains, y must give up to keep the sum at 4. It cuts the y-axis at (0, 4) and the x-axis at (4, 0).

**Takeaway to say out loud:** "Any pair that makes the rule true is on the picture — whatever shape the rule is written in."

---

## Charanam 6 — y = 2x + 3: the multiplier sets the steepness

**a. What we set up**

y = 2x + 3 — "double x, then add 3." (Writing `2x` is maths shorthand for 2 × x.)

| x | −2 | −1 | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | −1 | 1 | 3 | 5 | 7 |

**b. Task**

Plot the 5 points and join them. Then, with your pencil on any point of the line, move 1 step right — how many steps up must you climb to land back on the line? Check at two different points.

**c. Observation (what you should find)**

```graph
line -6.5 -10 3.5 10 | y = 2x + 3
dots -2 -1 -1 1 0 3 1 5 2 7
```

A straight line, but **steeper** than y = x: every 1 step right needs **2 steps up**. That 2 is exactly the number multiplying x. The `+ 3` tells you where the line cuts the y-axis: at (0, 3).

**Takeaway to say out loud:** "The number multiplying x is the climb for each step right."

---

## Charanam 7 — x = 3y + 2: feed the letter inside the rule

**a. What we set up**

x = 3y + 2 — this time the rule **computes x from y**. So do not choose x first — choose the letter that sits **inside** the arithmetic, y, and let the rule produce x:

| y | −2 | −1 | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| x | −4 | −1 | 2 | 5 | 8 |

Careful when plotting: the address is still written (x, y). The table's columns give the points (−4, −2), (−1, −1), (2, 0), (5, 1), (8, 2).

**b. Task**

Plot the 5 points and lay the ruler. Then check the step pattern: 1 step up costs how many steps right?

**c. Observation (what you should find)**

```graph
line -10 -4 10 2.667 | x = 3y + 2
dots -4 -2 -1 -1 2 0 5 1 8 2
```

A straight line again — a **gentle** one this time: 3 steps right for every 1 step up (the 3 that multiplies y). Choosing y first is not a trick; it is just the pallavi being practical — feed the rule the letter it wants, collect the other.

**Takeaway to say out loud:** "Feed the letter inside the rule; the lonely letter gets computed."

---

## Charanam 8 — y = x × x: the first rule that is not a line

**a. What we set up**

y = x × x — "y is x times itself" (x squared).

| x | −3 | −2 | −1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 9 | 4 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 4 | 9 |

Note the left half of the table: (−3) × (−3) = 9, because a minus times a minus gives a plus. So y is never negative.

**b. Task**

Plot all 7 points. Try the ruler: can one straight edge touch (1, 1), (2, 4) and (3, 9) together? Then join the points **freehand**, smoothly.

**c. Observation (what you should find)**

```graph
curve -3 9 -2 4 -1 1 0 0 1 1 2 4 3 9 | y = x × x
dots -3 9 -2 4 -1 1 0 0 1 1 2 4 3 9
```

The ruler fails — no straight edge holds these points. Joined smoothly, they form a **U-shaped curve**: falling to its lowest point (0, 0), then rising again, perfectly symmetric — x = 3 and x = −3 give the same y. The moment a rule multiplies **letter by letter**, the picture bends.

**Takeaway to say out loud:** "Letter times letter bends the picture."

---

## Charanam 9 — x = y × y + 2: a curve lying on its side

**a. What we set up**

x = y × y + 2 — the rule computes x from y (like Charanam 7), and it multiplies letter by letter (like Charanam 8). So: feed y, expect a curve.

| y | −2 | −1 | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| x | 6 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 6 |

The points, in (x, y) form: (6, −2), (3, −1), (2, 0), (3, 1), (6, 2).

**b. Task**

Plot the 5 points and join them smoothly. Which point is the "tip" of the shape? And notice: how many points does the curve have with x = 3?

**c. Observation (what you should find)**

```graph
curve 6 -2 3 -1 2 0 3 1 6 2 | x = y × y + 2
dots 6 -2 3 -1 2 0 3 1 6 2
```

The same U — but **lying on its side**, opening to the right, with its tip at (2, 0). It never reaches left of x = 2, because y × y is never negative, so x is never less than 2. And x = 3 happens **twice** — at y = 1 and at y = −1.

**Takeaway to say out loud:** "Same table method, whatever the rule — the shape is the rule's own signature."

## Practice on paper

For each rule: build the table, plot, join, and say whether it is a straight line or a curve. Check yourself against the final states below.

1. y = x − 1, for x = −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3
2. y = 3 and x = −2, both on one page — where do they cross?
3. x + y = 6, for x = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6
4. y = 2x − 1, for x = −1, 0, 1, 2, 3
5. x = 2y + 1, for y = −2, −1, 0, 1, 2
6. y = x × x + 1, for x = −2, −1, 0, 1, 2

**Final states for self-checking:**

**1.** y: −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2 — a straight line through (1, 0), climbing 1 up per 1 right.

```graph
line -9 -10 10 9 | y = x − 1
dots -2 -3 -1 -2 0 -1 1 0 2 1 3 2
```

**2.** A horizontal line and a vertical line, crossing at (−2, 3).

```graph
line -10 3 10 3 | y = 3
line -2 -10 -2 10 | x = −2
dots -2 3 | (−2, 3)
```

**3.** y: 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1, 0 — a straight downhill line from (0, 6) to (6, 0).

```graph
line -4 10 10 -4 | x + y = 6
dots 0 6 1 5 2 4 3 3 4 2 5 1 6 0
```

**4.** y: −3, −1, 1, 3, 5 — a straight line, 2 up per 1 right, cutting the y-axis at (0, −1).

```graph
line -4.5 -10 5.5 10 | y = 2x − 1
dots -1 -3 0 -1 1 1 2 3 3 5
```

**5.** x: −3, −1, 1, 3, 5 — points (−3, −2), (−1, −1), (1, 0), (3, 1), (5, 2); a straight line, 2 right per 1 up.

```graph

dots -3 -2 -1 -1 1 0 3 1 5 2
```

**6.** y: 5, 2, 1, 2, 5 — a U-shaped curve with its lowest point at (0, 1); never below it.

```graph
curve -3 10 -2 5 -1 2 0 1 1 2 2 5 3 10 | y = x × x + 1
dots -2 5 -1 2 0 1 1 2 2 5
```

## One-page reference

**The table method (works for every rule):**

| Step | Do this |
|---|---|
| 1 | Decide which letter to feed: the one **inside** the arithmetic. (For y = 2x + 3 feed x; for x = 3y + 2 feed y.) |
| 2 | Choose 5–7 easy values for it, including 0 and some negatives. |
| 3 | Compute the partner value with the rule — one careful arithmetic step per column. |
| 4 | Write each column as a point (x, y) — x first, **always**. |
| 5 | Plot the points; lay the ruler. All on one edge → join with the ruler. Not on one edge → join freehand, smoothly. |

**Reading a rule before drawing it:**

| If the rule… | The picture is… |
|---|---|
| uses x and y only with fixed numbers (×, +, −) | a **straight line** |
| says y = (a number), no x anywhere | a **horizontal** line at that height |
| says x = (a number), no y anywhere | a **vertical** line at that distance |
| multiplies letter by letter (x × x, y × y) | a **curve** — the ruler will refuse |

**Two quick checks:**

- **Ruler test:** plot 3 points; if one straight edge holds all 3 (and every further point too), it is a line.
- **Truth test:** to ask "is (a, b) on the picture?", put x = a, y = b into the rule — true means yes.
